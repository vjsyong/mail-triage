# Desktop critique — 2026-10-05

Critic: THE CRITIC, desktop pass. Method: 8 desktop screenshots (qa/shots/2026-10-05/desktop/, 1440x900) reviewed against `docs/ux-benchmarks.md` (read in full; cited as §n / Part 2 §n / [n]). Capability claims were checked against repo source (app.py templates, static JS), not vision alone; where only a still could answer, uncertainty is stated as such. 2 zoom crops used (messages rows, dashboard stats). No external fetches needed — all claims cite the benchmark doc or repo.

Screens judged: dashboard, messages, viewer, assistant, rules, settings, classifiers, accounts.

---

## Verdicts

- **Dashboard — par vs Vercel-style status surfaces (Part 2 §1):** Above par twice — the status card names state *and* freshness ("connected · checked 38s ago · next in <51s") rather than a bare dot, and every Recent filing is timestamped with its own Undo. Held at par because six of seven workload numbers are dead ends: only "need a reply" links anywhere ("view →"), against §1's rule that a stat card earns its box only if it leads somewhere.
- **Messages — par vs Gmail list anatomy (§1):** Row anatomy meets the bar (sender, subject + snippet, timestamp, status badges), six scoping chips beyond "All" (Awaiting LLM / Needs reply / Sorted / Tagged / Snoozed / Errors), page counts, and a real bulk bar (tag/untag/no-reply/classify — repo-verified). But the list has no search field (repo-verified: only the log and chat panes have search inputs), and no row carries an unread weight or dot — subjects are all normal-weight blue, so §1's "unmistakable unread affordance" is not met if NEW-badge is the intended signal.
- **Viewer — worse vs Shortwave/Fastmail reading pane (§3):** Newer/Older navigation, a real triage audit trail, and Classify/Tag actions exist — but the §3 bar is "next/prev + archive-and-advance + reply/reply-all/forward", and this pane has neither a reply affordance nor a file/move/mark-read action. The only primary button is "Classify with LLM" on a message that (per the list) the LLM already handles en masse. The pane shows mail beautifully and cannot act on it — the weakest screen of the eight.
- **Assistant — par vs Gmail Gemini sidebar / Shortwave chat (§5):** The concept is above par: a docked rail present on every screen with a live context chip ("Context: dashboard · message · rules list · classifiers list · settings") plus history and the promise "every step shows as it happens" is exactly §5's two-surface model (inline for the open message, chat for the mailbox). From stills alone I cannot verify the mutation gating (confirm + 60s-floor undo) that §5(c) makes the bar; the empty state is also a lot of whitespace above the input.
- **Rules — better vs Gmail filters / Outlook rules (§2):** The strongest screen. Ordered, numbered list with "first match wins" stated in the subtitle; guards (no-action rules = "keep in place") as a first-class concept, both at top; non-destructive per-rule toggles; and two dry-runs — "Test against last 200 messages" and "Simulate a draft" — that combine Outlook's run-on-existing [13] with Gmail's test-before-commit [11] into one surface. Reorder is ↑/↓ (Outlook's own pattern), so the Zapier drag bar [14] is unmet but the §2 bar ("reordering supported") is met.
- **Settings — par vs HIG settings grouping (Part 2 §5) / Gmail General (§7):** Task-named sections (General, AI Settings, Mail & connection, Sorting & filing), visible label + helper text on every field, and current values shown (offset 8 → "timestamps show as UTC+8"; model, base URL). Off: the left sub-nav (General, AI Settings, Search Index, System status…) reads as page navigation but sections stack into one scroll — you can't match a sub-nav item to what's in the viewport — and per-section saves have no page-level dirty cue.
- **Classifiers — par vs Part 2 §2 tooling bar / Shortwave AI filters (§2, §5):** Genuinely more transparent than any commercial peer fetched in the doc — decision-list internals are shown inline ("body contains … → __other__ (100%, n=95)"), and labeling training data "WEAK" admits LLM-derived labels are soft. But the surface doesn't let you judge a classifier: "WEAK", "min conf 0.80" and "-1 removed" are undefined here (some are explained on the dataset page), and there is no accuracy/precision anywhere in view. (Match-rule cells look long; I am not claiming clipping.)
- **Accounts — par vs §6 sync/status bar:** State naming is better than typical — PROXY RUNNING with pid + uptime, then AUTHORISED and READING as separate badges, token expiry, "loopback mode". But §6's bar ("offline/cached scope stated") is unmet on the surface: nothing says how far back the app reads or what's cached; that lives inside the collapsed "Setup details", and there's no direct Test-connection affordance next to AUTHORISED.

## Top gaps vs the commercial bar

1. **The viewer can't act on the mail it shows.** Gap: no reply / reply-all / forward, no file-to-folder, no mark-read in the reading pane — triage actions are Classify and Tag only. Who does it best: Shortwave (assistant acts on "any email on your screen" [AI docs]) and Fastmail (`[`/`]` archive-and-next, single-key reply [Shortcuts]). Why it matters: this app's headline concept is the "Needs reply" queue (§2 bar: "a linear needs-reply queue") — yet the one screen where you read a needs-reply email offers no way to reply, so the loop dead-ends and the workflow must detour through the assistant chat.
2. **No keyboard layer or command palette.** Gap: repo search finds no shortcut system — the only global key handler is Escape (disarm), plus Enter in comboboxes. Who does it best: Superhuman (Cmd/Ctrl+K = "your master control for any action") and Fastmail (j/k/x/d/y/z, 10-action undo). Why it matters: §2's bar is ≥5 single-key actions; a triage tool that auto-sorts 99% of mail is a throughput tool, and mouse-only caps the ceiling of the core loop (list → read → dispose → next).
3. **No search on the message list.** Gap: finding a specific message means paging through 37 pages or asking the assistant — keyword search exists only in the log and chat filters (repo-verified). Who does it best: Gmail/Fastmail — search is on every list. Why it matters: §1's list is a triage surface, but retrieval ("find that invoice") is the other half of mail UX; semantic-only search is a single point of failure and costs a chat round-trip to use.
4. **Dashboard stats are dead ends.** Gap: 6 of 7 workload numbers link nowhere; "0 need a reply · view →" is the lone exception. Who does it best: Vercel-style status/dashboard surfaces (Part 2 §1: stats "answer one question and lead somewhere" [23][26]) — and this app already proves it knows the pattern. Why it matters: numbers a user can't open force them to reconstruct the queue by hand (which filter? which page?) — the dashboard stops being a doorway and becomes a poster.
5. **Undo stops at recent filings.** Gap: per-filing Undo on the dashboard is excellent, but nothing visible covers the assistant's or the user's own mutations; the dashboard line scopes it ("Undo puts one back and keeps automation off it"). Who does it best: Fastmail (`z` — undo up to the last 10 actions [Shortcuts]) and Gmail Gemini (60s undo on confirmed agentic actions [Gemini help]). Why it matters: §5(c) makes confirm + undo the trust floor for an app that mutates mail; when the app files thousands of messages by rule and LLM, undo reachability is what keeps the "never deletes mail" guarantee credible. (If undo exists elsewhere, these 8 shots don't show it — phrased as a visibility gap.)

## Nits

1. **major** — Messages — toolbar: no search input anywhere on the list (repo-verified) — §1 bar / Gmail-Fastmail list anatomy — add a search box to the toolbar row.
2. **minor** — Messages — subject column: unread has no weight or dot; all visible subjects are normal-weight blue and some rows show no badge at all (crop-verified) — §1 "unmistakable unread affordance" — bold unread subjects + dot, or document the read model (possibly deliberate: NEW may mean newly-arrived, not unread — uncertain).
3. **nit** — Messages — TAG column: empty on every visible row yet consumes width — §1 density / §8 craft — auto-hide the column when the page has no tags.
4. **minor** — Messages — page description: "select rows to tag, classify or file in bulk" promises filing, but the bulk bar (repo-verified) is Tag / Untag / No reply needed / Classify selected — no file/move action — Part 2 §8 (copy should match capability) — add a bulk file/move action or reword the description.
5. **major** — Viewer — action rail: the only primary button is "Classify with LLM"; no Reply / Forward / File / mark-read (see gap 1) — §3 bar — put Reply and File-to-folder in the header action row; keep classify secondary.
6. **minor** — Viewer — Details rail: narrow rail wraps the Message-ID across several lines and squeezes the body column — Part 2 §1 (card scan) — collapse Details into a disclosure; show the ID on demand.
7. **nit** — Viewer — audit-trail empty state: "Backfill from stored state" is machine jargon; the §8 empty-state recipe (title + reason + action) is otherwise met — reword to user terms ("Rebuild this email's history").
8. **minor** — Dashboard — workload card: stats are non-interactive text and "% of 3610 seen" uses the app's own "seen" noun unglossed — Part 2 §1 (each stat answers one question and leads somewhere) — make each stat link to its filtered list; spell out "seen".
9. **nit** — Rules — guard rows: a guard differs from an acting rule only in the ACTIONS text ("keep in place (guard)") — §8 (states deliberate) — give guard rows a distinct badge so protection vs action reads at a scan.
10. **minor** — Settings — left sub-nav vs content: the sub-nav reads as page navigation but sections stack in one scroll, so nav items can't be matched to the viewport — Part 2 §5 (grouping by the task it changes) — split into pages or add scroll-spy highlighting.
11. **minor** — Classifiers — table: "WEAK", "min conf 0.80", "-1 removed" are undefined on this surface and no accuracy/precision is shown — §3 (state in color *and* text) + §8 (thresholds deliberate) — add tooltips/legend and precision/recall per classifier.
12. **minor** — Accounts — account card: sync scope/history depth invisible (collapsed "Setup details"); no test affordance beside AUTHORISED — §6 (state what is available) — surface "reads last N days · folders 20/20" and add a Test button.

## Summary

1. Rules is the standout — dry-runs, guards, and ordering beat both Gmail and Outlook as shipped; Accounts and the Assistant concept also read above par.
2. The viewer is the failure: a triage app whose reading pane can't reply, file, or mark read — "worse" vs the §3 bar, and the cheapest fix with the biggest payoff.
3. Three table-stakes affordances are simply absent: list search, a keyboard layer, and stat drill-downs — each cited to §1/§2/Part 2 §1.
4. Trust is well-instrumented (timestamps everywhere, per-filing undo, audit trails, "never deletes mail" copy) but doesn't yet reach assistant/user actions — gap 5.
5. Classifier transparency exceeds commercial peers; its state language (WEAK, min conf, negative sample counts) needs a legend before it means anything.
