## Verdicts (one line per screen)

- **dashboard — better vs the Vercel-style dashboard bar (P2 §1):** the hero answers exactly one question ("934 — need a reply"), links to the filtered list it summarizes ("view →" / "Open messages"), the status card names state, "Last check 19s ago" + "Check now" give the freshness/manual-refresh pair, and every "recent" row is timestamped. Only faults: two inert stat rows and hover-only chip explanations (nits below).
- **messages — worse vs the Gmail/Spark list bar (P1 §1):** scoping axes pass comfortably (All / Awaiting LLM / Needs reply / Moved / Tagged beats the "≥2 beyond All" bar) and the classification chips (SORTED, FLOW, LLM → FOLDER, Notification 95%) answer "where did it go" better than any consumer list; but the row anatomy fails two of five bar items — no unread affordance of any kind, no snippet on most rows — and "page 1 of 36" puts pagination where every peer ships a continuous list.
- **viewer — worse vs the Fastmail reading-pane bar (P1 §3):** Fastmail's pane carries its own vocabulary (j/k, `[`/`]` archive-and-next, r/a/f reply keys); ours renders one message with SORTED / MOVE:CANVAS / "View plain text" — no next/prev, no archive-and-advance anywhere on the screen, and the reply composer sits below the fold. Triage here still bounces back to the list per message.
- **assistant — par vs Shortwave's conversational surface (P1 §5):** icon + one-line purpose + four contextual starters + composer is the right empty state, and the app's own copy claims semantic archive search, live-streamed tool steps, and rule proposals "you approve with one click" — i.e. at the Shortwave bar on paper. A still cannot verify the two things the bar actually demands (confirm + undo on mutations); QA must exercise them directly.
- **rules — better vs the Outlook/Gmail rule-editor bar (P2 §2):** ordered numbered rules, per-rule reorder, non-destructive "disable", and — rare — a run-on-existing dry run ("Test against last 200 messages") exposed before any commit. This is the app's strongest screen and it beats the commercial defaults on the exact axis the benchmark calls out (testing is hidden behind a commit in most peers).
- **settings — par vs the Apple HIG settings model (P2 §5):** task-named tabs, every setting showing its current value, each with a one-sentence explanation of what it does — textbook. The weak spot is a raw decimal timezone field where peers offer a zone picker.
- **more — par vs HIG (P2 §5):** long-tail destinations (Rules, Flows, Classifiers, Templates / Accounts, Log, Settings) live in More exactly as HIG prescribes, grouping is task-named, and the footer even states "Times in UTC+8". Nothing needs to move.
- **log — worse vs Vercel runtime logs (P2 §3):** newest-first timestamps, level filter chips (Errors 8 / Warnings 19 / Info), a debug toggle, and a monospace black panel with text+color severity all pass; the bar's other half — search, time window, pauseable live mode — is absent.

## Top gaps vs the commercial bar

1. **There is no undo anywhere.** Rules move mail, the LLM auto-files it, bulk buttons classify it — and the app has no undo affordance (grep across the repo: no "undo" or "snooze" in `app.py`; the project's own `agent-permissions.md` lists "undo queue for applied actions" as *out of scope v1*; a tool description even says "Deleting cannot be undone").
   *Best:* Fastmail's 10-action undo stack and server-side 20s send hold; Gmail's 60s undo on Gemini's agentic actions (P1 §2, §4, §5 bars).
   *Why it matters here:* the product's core promise is that a machine files your mail for you. Every wrong move currently costs a manual hunt through folders; without one-tap reversal, trust in the automation — the whole product — erodes.
2. **The reading pane cannot run the triage loop.** No next/prev, no archive-and-advance, no "reply to this one, then the next" mode; reply is below the fold.
   *Best:* Fastmail (`[`/`]` archive-and-move-on) and HEY's Focus & Reply ("knock out 5 of 12, or all 12, or none") (P1 §3 bar).
   *Why it matters here:* "Needs reply 934" is a queue in name only if the only way to drain it is back-navigate → find row → open → scroll. Triage = decide → act → next, and that loop isn't on this screen.
3. **The list can't express state.** A row shows sender, subject, time, and machine chips — but not read/unread, not "handled", and only sometimes a snippet. Read items don't recede (P1 §1 bar).
   *Best:* Gmail/Spark's unread-weight + seen-section model; HEY's Imbox (only what matters) (P1 §1 bar).
   *Why it matters here:* with 3,559 messages and 99% LLM coverage, the list is the surface where the user verifies what the machine did. Today the only navigators are filter counts; every row looks equally "new".
4. **No deferral primitive.** No snooze, no reminders, no "reply later" pile — grep confirms both are absent from the codebase, not just the shots.
   *Best:* Gmail/Spark/Shortwave snooze presets; Superhuman Auto Reminders; HEY's Reply Later + Bubble Up (P1 §2 bar).
   *Why it matters here:* 934 messages "need a reply" and the only verbs are *do it now* or *file it away*. Deferral is how a triage tool survives a backlog — rules can't express "bring this back Friday".
5. **The log is a viewer, not a diagnostic tool.** Level chips + debug toggle only: no search, no time window, no pause for a page that refreshes under you; severity is text+badge (good), and timestamps have no zone on the page itself.
   *Best:* Vercel runtime logs — level + time window + route/status search + pauseable live mode, UTC stated (P2 §3 bar; pause also WCAG 2.2 2.2.2, P2 §7).
   *Why it matters here:* when auto-filing misfires (and at 99% coverage it will), this log is the only forensics. Without search/time filtering, a 30-second check becomes scroll archaeology through "emailproxy started (pid 12)".

## Summary

- The strongest surfaces are the ones the benchmark treats as hard: rules (ordered, toggleable, with a real dry run) comfortably beats the Outlook/Gmail editor bar, and the dashboard hero clears every P2 §1 target.
- The weakest is the triage loop itself: the list shows what the machine did but not what *you've* done, the viewer can't advance a queue, and nothing anywhere can be undone — for an automation-first mail app that is the trust gap, not a polish gap.
- Matched-to-correct: assistant, settings, and More all sit at their respective bars; the log and viewer need the missing-controls work (search/window/pause; next/prev/archive).
- Highest-leverage single change: an undo layer for applied actions (rule moves, LLM filings, bulk classify) — it closes the app's biggest gap, the log's audit trail becomes actionable, and "agent permissions" out-of-scope item comes in-scope.
- Sources honored: P1/P2 of `docs/ux-benchmarks.md` throughout; absence claims (undo, snooze, archive, next/prev) verified against the repo, not inferred from stills.
