## Verdicts — one line per screen

- **Dashboard — better vs Part 2 §1 status-card bar ([23][26][16], Vercel/Linear-style):** all three hero stats deep-link to the filtered queues they summarize (2485-2487), status chips name state + one action (2490-2493), freshness is always visible ("Last check 34s ago", 1139), Recent-mail rows carry timestamps and labeled LLM badges — no KPI tile answers nothing, no card-on-card, no untimestamped "recent". Passes the bar's hard parts.
- **Messages — worse than Gmail mobile / Spark Smart Inbox (Part 1 §1–2):** 7 count-bearing scope chips (6446-6449), totals + pagination (6362-6368), 24px bulk select and a contextual sticky bulk bar (6323-6333) are par-or-better; but rows carry no per-row action and no seen/arrival state, and a dominating sender never collapses to one row (HEY) — the classifier's "new/queued/sorted" badges (382-392) describe the machine, never the human.
- **Viewer — par vs Gmail's reading pane / HEY Focus-&-Reply (Part 1 §3):** Newer/Older + "File & next" auto-advance (6682-6688), Draft reply / Snooze (1d/3d/1w) / Needs reply (6691-6694), LLM summary + category correction (6723), and honest "drafts land in Drafts, nothing sent automatically" (6698); misses: actions sticky at the top (6660) rather than bottom-docked, and no reply-all/forward.
- **Assistant — par vs Shortwave's assistant / Gmail Gemini sidebar (Part 1 §5):** taught empty state with six one-tap prompts (7550-7556), chats saved and resumable (7589), and a permission spec that literally lists "Can do directly / Asks first / Off" (7660-7662) — mutation-gating transparency is at or above most peers; the raw model chip is the only wart.
- **Rules — better than Gmail filters / Outlook rules as experienced on a phone (Part 2 §2 [11][13][15]):** numbered ordered rows with mono condition + action summary, non-destructive toggles, a real dry-run ("Test against last 200 messages", 2892) and "Simulate a draft" (2893), precision ⤒↑↓ buttons instead of drag (WCAG 2.5.7; research-lists §/rules) — Gmail can't even build a filter on mobile web.
- **Settings — par vs Gmail/Spark settings structure (Part 1 §7, Part 2 §5):** five task-named tabs, every control shows its current value with label-above (8899-8900), 16px inputs kill iOS focus-zoom (930-931), savebar is keyboard-lifted via visualViewport (2147); HIG's "infrequent config only" is respected.
- **More — par vs HIG §5 "More-menu" pattern (Part 2 §5 [7], mobile brief §1 combo-nav):** long-tail destinations grouped AUTOMATION/SYSTEM, 54px rows with descriptions + chevrons (2252-2282); "Get started" carries the onboarding contract (Part 1 §8).
- **Log — par vs Vercel Runtime Logs (Part 2 §3 [16][22][3]):** level chips with counts, 15m/1h/24h windows, search + Find, persisted Pause (10255-10268), newest-first mono rows with text+color severity (10273); every "THE BAR" item is present — the single-row toolbar is the one place it skimps.

## Top gaps vs the commercial bar

1. **Rows can't be acted on from the list.** Best: Gmail (configurable swipe incl. None), Spark (one swipe: read/pin/archive/delete/snooze), Superhuman. Why it matters: with 3,629 messages the queue-cost of "open → act → advance" is 2+ taps per message, and the bulk bar only helps batches; one visible per-row action (e.g. File & next, with a button path per WCAG 2.5.7) would turn the list into the triage surface the other apps make it.
2. **No human "seen/arrived" state.** Best: Gmail's unmistakable unread affordance, Spark's new-on-top/Seen-recede, HEY's Imbox. We show *machine* state only (new/queued/sorted/kept, 382-392); a message you read and one you never opened render identically once classified. The dashboard's Recent card is the only recency surface.
3. **Undo is off the point of action.** Best: Gmail/Superhuman undo toast, Fastmail's 10-action stack (Part 1 §2 bar: "global undo (toast or ~10-action stack)"). Our undo exists per-filing on Dashboard → Recent filings (2630-2640) while the act itself only flashes "Filed to X — next up." (7429) — after a 50-message pass, fixing one mistake means finding it on another screen.
4. **Freshness contract stops short on list surfaces.** Best: Superhuman/Gmail offline-and-sync contracts (Part 1 §6). We have the visible half (topbar "Last check Ns ago" + dot, 1139) but the only manual refresh is Dashboard "Check now" (2469, 2624) and Log's Refresh (10268); the messages list has no refresh control and no offline/pending story.
5. **Snooze/queue vocabulary coarser than peers.** Best: Spark/Superhuman snooze presets (today/tomorrow/weekend/next week/custom — Part 1 §2 bar) and Superhuman Auto Reminders for unanswered mail (vendor claim). We offer only 1d/3d/1w (6693) and nothing that resurfaces a "Needs reply" after N days — for a triage app the reply-later queue is the product.

## Summary

- Strongest screens: **Rules** (better than any mobile peer — ordered, dry-run, non-destructive), **Dashboard** (all status-bar items pass), **Log** (every Vercel-bar filter present), and the **Assistant**'s permission transparency.
- Weakest: the **Messages list** — no row-level triage, no human seen/new state, no sender bundling; and **undo** lives one screen away from where mail gets filed.
- The app is confidently "par" almost everywhere vs named peers; the remaining gaps are action-model gaps (list triage, undo-at-action, refresh contract), not visual ones.
- Two claims to verify on device: viewer workbench occlusion under the sticky topbar (nit 2), and no per-row triage for real users (gap 1).
